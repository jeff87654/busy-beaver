# Pitfalls of this development (and a few sandbox notes)

## 1. `execute` is cubic in the number of steps

busycoq's `execute` tactic runs the machine until the goal is reached; its
cost grows like the *cube* of the number of steps, because every step
rebuilds the tape term.  The start of this machine is only 9 steps long and
is cut into chunks of 5 (`BB54_start.v` is generated by `../tools/gen_start.py`),
which is enough; for the BB(3,4) champion, with 241 steps, the chunks have
to be 10 steps long.

## 2. `g 4` must never be evaluated

`g k m` is defined by

    Fixpoint g (k m : nat) : nat :=
      match k with
      | O => S m
      | S k' => Nat.iter (2 * m + 2) (g k') 0%nat
      end.

`g 4 x` iterates `g 3` `2x+2` times, so *normalising* `g 4 x` also
normalises its argument, and the normal form is astronomically large.  Any
tactic that tries to evaluate it (`simpl`, `vm_compute`, `lia` on a goal
that mentions it) will either diverge or exhaust the stack.

The development therefore keeps the score-carrying number behind a `sig`:

    Lemma g40_spec : {x : nat | x = Nat.iter 1%nat (g 4%nat) 0%nat}.
    Definition g40 : nat := proj1_sig g40_spec.
    Lemma g40_eq : g40 = Nat.iter 1%nat (g 4%nat) 0%nat.

`g40` is opaque for every tactic, and the closed form `2 * g40 + 4 = 2 ↑^3 4`
is proved separately (`g40_closed`), by rewriting.

The same holds for the Knuth arrows: `knuth n a b` is never evaluated either,
it is only manipulated by rewriting (`knuth_S` and friends).

## 3. The kernel must never *reduce* a big number

This is the same pitfall as in section 2, seen from the other side: the
kernel reduces a fixpoint whenever it has to, and the normal form of `g 4 x`
is astronomically large.  Two rules keep it away:

* every arithmetic step is a `rewrite` (which replaces a term by a
  syntactically equal one) and never a conversion (`reflexivity` on two
  different terms would reduce them);
* the rewrites that mention a big number are stated for *variables* and
  instantiated afterwards, because a `rewrite` inspects the candidates of
  the goal and the kernel reduces them while it does so.

Concretely, the counts of the run are always written in the shape `S (...)`
and never as `2 * ... + 1`: the latter is a `Nat.add` whose first argument is
stuck, and the kernel will try to reduce it.

## 4. Three more pitfalls of the halting proof (`BB54_halt.v`)
The kernel is much more eager than one would like, and the following three
rules were needed to make the proof go through.  They are worth stating,
because each of them cost a long debugging session.

**(a) The kernel reduces a fixpoint as soon as it has to.**  Comparing two
terms that are not syntactically equal makes it unfold them -- and
unfolding `g 5 (g 4 0)` means evaluating `g 4 0`, which needs `2^116`
iterations.  So the two sides of every comparison must be *literally* the
same term.  The counts are therefore always written in the shape
`S (...)` and never as `2 * ... + 1`: the latter is a `Nat.add` whose first
argument is stuck, and the kernel will try to reduce it.

**(b) `Stream` is coinductive, and the kernel unfolds cofixes.**  Two tape
terms that differ syntactically make the kernel unfold `pat13`, `pat31` and
-- inside them -- the `g`'s.  So every stream that occurs is written in
exactly the form the corresponding lemma produces.

**(c) A `Definition` is unfolded by the kernel, a `Notation` is not.**  The
big numbers are therefore *notations*:

    Notation X  := (g 5 (g 4 0)).
    Notation M0 := (gchain (2 * X) 3 0).
    Notation M1 := (gchain (2 * M0) (S (X + 1)) 0).

A notation is expanded when the term is parsed, so the kernel never sees the
name.  With a `Definition` the kernel would unfold `M1` whenever it compares
it with the `gchain` term of a lemma, and then reduce the `gchain` -- and
that reduction digs into `g`.

Two further details:

* where a big number occurs *inside a list* (`[S3]^^(2 * M1)`) it must be
  opaque even for the kernel's own type checker, which has to compute the
  length of the block; this is what the sealed aliases `M0s`, `M1s` of
  `BB54_halt.v` are for (`Opaque`);
* every arithmetic step is a `rewrite`, never a conversion, and the
  rewrites that mention the big numbers are stated for *variables*
  (`count_final_gen`, `sigma_final_eq_gen`) and instantiated afterwards.
  A `rewrite` looks at the candidates of the goal, and the kernel reduces
  them while it does so.

## 5. The busycoq tape conventions are easy to get wrong

The left stream of a tape is listed **from the head outwards**, and the two
notations for the head differ by one element:

* `l {{Q}}> r` puts the head on the first cell of `r`; `l` is the *whole*
  left context;
* `l <{{Q}} r` puts the head on the first cell of `l`; `tl l` is the left
  context.

So `([2]^^k *> [3]^^m *> const 0) <{{B}} r` has `k+1` twos to the left of the
head (the head sits on one of them), while
`([2]^^k *> [3]^^m *> const 0) {{B}}> r` has exactly `k` twos to the left.

This is the reason the arguments of `Cor2` and `turnaround` look shifted by
one with respect to the ones of `Bcfg`; a proof that "does not apply" is
usually off by one there.

Two more small points:

* the numerals `0`, `1`, `2`, `3` are *notations for the symbols* `S0`..`S3`
  in this development, so a `nat` position needs `%nat` and a symbol
  position needs `S3` explicitly;
* `Nat.pow_succ_r` in Coq 8.20 has a side condition `0 <= n`, so
  `rewrite Nat.pow_succ_r` leaves a second goal behind.

## 6. Arrow arithmetic without evaluating anything (`BB54_bound.v`)

The runtime bounds of the wiki are towers of Knuth arrows, and the identity
that ties them to the machine,

    2 ↑^4 2 ↑^3 4  =  2X + 4        (X = g_5 (g_4 0)),

must be proved without ever unfolding a `g` or a `knuth`.  Two devices make
this possible.

**The closed forms are stated for variables.**  `g_closed` and
`g_iter_closed` of `BB54_score.v` come in a *general* form,

    Lemma g_closed : forall k m, 2 * g (S k) m + 4 = knuth k 2 (2 * m + 4).
    Lemma g_iter_closed_gen : forall k n x,
      x = Nat.iter n (g k) 0 -> 2 * x + 4 = knuth k 2 (n + 2).

so that they can be instantiated at a concrete level (`k = 4`, `k = 3`)
without the `g` on the left ever being unfolded.  `g_iter_closed_gen` in
particular takes the iteration count and the starting point as *hypotheses*,
which is what lets `g 4 0` (an unfolded `g`!) appear on the left.

**The arrows are only ever rewritten.**  `knuth_S` is the recursion of the
arrows,

    knuth (S n) a (S b) = knuth n a (knuth (S n) a b),

i.e. `a ↑^(n+1) (b+1) = a ↑^n (a ↑^(n+1) b)`.  Chained, it turns

    knuth 3 2 4  =  knuth 2 2 65536        (2 ↑^3 4 = 2 ↑↑ 65536)
    knuth 4 2 3  =  knuth 3 2 4            (2 ↑^4 3 = 2 ↑^3 4)

into a sequence of rewrites.  The inner applications then reduce to *small*
numerals only — `knuth 3 2 0 = 1`, `knuth 2 2 1 = 2`, `knuth 2 2 2 = 4`,
`knuth 2 2 4 = 65536`, four doublings — while the outermost `Nat.iter` keeps
its tower-shaped count and is never evaluated; the two sides of the goal are
then *literally* the same term, and `reflexivity` has nothing left to do.

Note that the instances of `knuth_S` have to be chosen to match the shape of the
goal exactly: `knuth_S 2 2 3` is the step from `knuth 3 2 4` to
`knuth 2 2 (knuth 3 2 3)`, and writing `knuth_S 1 2 2` instead leaves a goal
with no matching subterm.

## 7. Comparing towers of different bases (`BB54_tower.v`)

The comparison `10 ↑↑ 65533 < 2 ↑↑ 65536` decides the direction of the two
runtime bounds, and it is proved in Coq — with the following three points in
mind.

**The induction needs a margin.**  The statement `10 ↑↑ n < 2 ↑↑ (n+3)` does
*not* propagate from `n` to `n+1`: the step turns `10 ↑↑ n` into
`10^(10 ↑↑ n) ≤ 16^(10 ↑↑ n) = 2^(4 · 10 ↑↑ n)`, and that has to be compared
with `2 ↑↑ (n+4) = 2^(2 ↑↑ (n+3))`, which needs `4 · 10 ↑↑ n` to be smaller
than `2 ↑↑ (n+3)` — a quarter of it.  The invariant that *does* propagate is

    4 · (10 ↑↑ n)  +  4   ≤   2 ↑↑ (n+3)      (tower10_inv)

because the same computation then gives `4 · 10 ↑↑ (n+1) + 4 ≤ 4 · 2^(T-4) + 4
≤ 2^T` with `T = 2 ↑↑ (n+3)`.  The margin of 4 is exactly what pays for the
factor 4 of the base change `10 < 2^4`.

**`rewrite` with a `≤` lemma is a setoid rewrite.**  `rewrite H` where `H` is
an inequality fails with

    setoid rewrite failed: UNDEFINED EVARS: ...

because the rewrite has to go through the `Proper` instances of the
relation.  The way out is to leave the hypothesis in the context and let
`lia` do the work — it treats `2 ^ (knuth 2 2 (S (S (S n))))` as an opaque
atom and chains the hypotheses.  Rewriting with an *equality* lemma inside a
`≤` hypothesis is fine.

**`Nat.pow_succ_r'` puts the factor on the left.**  It reads
`a ^ S b = a * a ^ b`, so `(2 ^ k) ^ S x` becomes `2 ^ k * 2 ^ (k * x)` — the
shorter exponent comes *first*.  `Nat.pow_add_r` is
`a ^ (b + c) = a ^ b * a ^ c`, so the two fit together only after a
`replace ... by lia` that commutes the addition.

## 8. Sandbox notes (only relevant on FUSE/"oss"-style mounts)

On some network mounts the `coqc` process sees a very slow `stat`, which
makes the compilation of the busycoq dependencies painful; compiling the
files one by one in dependency order (as `compile_clean.sh` does) is much
faster than one big `coqc` invocation.  Also, a compile check must not pipe
`coqc` into `head`: `$?` would then be the status of `head`, not of `coqc`.
Use

    coqc ... > log 2>&1; echo EXIT=$?
